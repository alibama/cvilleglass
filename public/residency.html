Artist residency · 2026

Our first residency: Glass Houses.

Charlottesville Glass has awarded its first artist residency to Hanna Taubenberger, a sculptor who builds steel forms for glass. Three days in the hot shop, two mentors at the bench, and a new series of work.

The artist

Hanna Taubenberger.

Hanna works in steel. She fabricates the forms that glass sculptures are built around — the frames, cages, and housings that give molten glass somewhere to go and something to push against. The residency brought that steel into the hot shop, where hot glass met cold metal and the two materials had to learn to hold each other.

The result is Glass Houses, a series of sculptures made during her three days with us. Follow her work on Instagram at @hannacanvas →

  • 3 daysin the hot shop
  • Steel + glassforms built for the furnace
  • 2 mentorsat the bench throughout

The residency

Three days at Raging Buffalo Glass Studio.

The residency took place at Raging Buffalo Glass Studio, with mentorship and hands-on assistance from Anson Parker and Chip Hall, who oversaw the creation of the Glass Houses series from first gather to the annealer.

That is what a residency here is for. Glassblowing is a partnership at 1,900°F, and an artist arriving from another material needs people who can run the fire, read the glass, and keep a piece alive while the idea gets worked out. Our mentors do that, so the resident can stay with the work.

Why residencies

Working artists make the studio better for everyone.

Our classes and sessions are built for beginners, and they always will be. But a studio also needs people pushing the material past what it’s done before. A residency gives an artist from outside glass the time, the fire, and the hands to try something ambitious — and it gives everyone who walks through the door a look at what the material can become.

Residents bring new techniques and new questions into the hot shop. The skills and the know-how stay here, in Charlottesville, passed hand to hand.

What a residency includes

Studio time

Dedicated days in the hot shop, with the furnace, glory hole, and annealer run for you.

Mentorship

Experienced glassblowers at the bench throughout — teaching, assisting, and keeping the piece alive.

A body of work

You leave with finished pieces, and we help share the work with the community.

Future residencies

Interested in the next one?

We’re a new organization and this was our first residency. More will follow as funding and studio time allow. If you’re an artist — in any material — with an idea that needs hot glass, tell us about it. If you’d like to help fund the next resident’s time at the bench, we’d love that too.

Good to know

No glass experience needed.

Hanna came to the residency as a steel fabricator, not a glassblower. That’s the point: our mentors handle the glass side so a resident can bring their own material, their own practice, and their own questions to the fire.
